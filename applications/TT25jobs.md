## Deciding when and where to apply {#whenandwhere}


I originally planned on going on the market the previous year, but a job in an industry research group landed in my lap.
The general consensus among the people I talked to was that, as long as I was still reasonably research-active,  being out of academia for a year or two wouldn't be a big barrier to getting a faculty job.
I decided to take the job which I figured was a good hedge against the variance of the academic job market, would let me stay in NYC, and would give me a chance to learn about computational science in industry.
However, after a year and some unexpected/unforseeable changes to the job description, it was clear that it was time for me to move on.

The main constraint in my job search was geography; I was very set on staying in NYC or moving to Shanghai.
I applied for basically every open position in Math and Computer Science in NYC and a few positions in Shanghai.
This included a variety of types of instuitutions, ranging from undergrad teaching-heavy positions, research institutes, large reserach univerisites, and NYU Shanghai.


## Responses {#response}

I heard about first-round interviews in mid Febraury, which seemed very late compared to past years. 
I recieved my official offer from NYU Shanghai in mid April.

## Reflections {#reflection}

I've been interested in NYU Shanghai since my PhD and made a number of deliberate career decisions (including taking my postdoc at NYU) with this in mind.
So the fact that I actually got the job is a bit surreal. 

More broadly, I'm relieved to be done with job searches and am looking forward to the stability the new job provides.
Finding a faculty position has been a lurking stressor for the past decade, and while I enjoyed getting to see many different cities, the constant uncertainty of what's next was draining.
While my tenure evaluation (6 years from now) is still ahead and the impact of AI on the discipline is uncertain, I feel much more at ease about my career than I have at any point prior.
